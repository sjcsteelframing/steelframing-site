Studio units as a boarding house: is it worth changing the business model?

Registering a building of studio units as a boarding house does not, by itself, guarantee a 6% tax rate. The classification only holds if the activity is genuinely lodging — and that changes the contract, the permits, the design and the operating costs.

Overview

The decision that comes before the design

Building studio units — kitnets, as they are called in Brazil — for rent looks simple: land, design, construction and rent. But one earlier decision can change whether the project works: will the property be run as a residential lease or as a lodging service?

The same floor plan can serve both models. The difference shows up in the contract, taxes, permits and costs, and in requirements of the design itself, such as accessibility and fire safety.

To answer whether the change is worth it, this report looks at three things: the nature of the activity, its legal classification and the financial result. It covers Brazilian law and taxes; amounts are in Brazilian reais (R$).

1 · Concepts

A residential studio and a boarding house are different businesses

In a lease, the investor grants the use of the property; in lodging, the investor provides a service that includes accommodation. That difference decides which law applies, which tax is due and how much it costs to operate.

  • Residential lease. The investor makes the unit available, as a rule under a contract governed by the Tenancy Law. Revenue is the rent; electricity, water, condominium fees and property tax (IPTU) are usually paid by the tenant.
  • Boarding house or lodging. The business provides accommodation with services — cleaning, bed linen, internet, front desk and sometimes meals. The price is a single amount that already includes them.

Length of stay alone does not define the activity. What counts is the nature of the contract, the services actually provided and the license to operate.

Brazil’s Supreme Federal Court reinforced this distinction when it ruled on ADI 5764 (2023): a lodging relationship is not the same as a property lease. That is why a furnished studio rented by the month does not become a boarding house just by renaming the contract.

2 · Law

What does the Tenancy Law say?

Law No. 8,245/1991 only excludes establishments that meet two conditions at the same time: they provide regular services and they are licensed to operate as such. Without both, occupancy tends to be treated as a lease.

  • Art. 1, sole paragraph, item a, 4. Leases in apart-hotels, residence hotels or equivalent establishments remain governed by the Civil Code and special laws — “understood as those that provide regular services to their users and are licensed to operate as such.” Boarding houses are not named: if they qualify, it is as an “equivalent” establishment, which depends on how they actually operate.
  • Art. 2, sole paragraph. Occupants of collective multi-family dwellings are presumed to be tenants or subtenants. A building with several units occupied by long-term residents, with no services and no lodging license, falls under this presumption.
  • Art. 45. Lease clauses intended to defeat the purposes of the law are void. Calling the contract “lodging” does not strip the occupant of tenant protections, such as eviction only through the courts.

Quotations from statutes are free translations; the Portuguese text of the law prevails.

The longer the stay and the leaner the service, the more the operation looks like a lease — whatever the contract is called.
3 · Classification

What makes a boarding house?

Brazil’s official activity classification (CNAE) has two possible codes, and student boarding houses appear under the second. Neither code, on its own, authorizes the business to operate.

CNAE codeWhat it coversSimples Nacional
5590-6/03 — Boarding houses (pensões)Accommodation in boarding houses, with or without mealsAllowed, Annex III, no Factor R
5590-6/99 — Other accommodationIn the IBGE notes: non-tourist collective accommodation, such as student houses and boarding houses; also short-term rental of residential propertyAllowed, Annex III, no Factor R

Serving meals is not required to use 5590-6/03. The accountant chooses the code based on the actual operation.

The General Tourism Law (Law No. 11,771/2008, as amended by Law No. 14,978/2024) defines a lodging establishment by the whole package: temporary accommodation, units for the guest’s exclusive use, services, an express or implied contract and a daily rate (art. 23). For those that qualify:

  • Cadastur, the federal tourism registry, is mandatory (art. 22), free and valid for two years; operating without it leads to a warning and a fine (art. 41);
  • booking platforms may only list registered lodging establishments (art. 22, § 6);
  • the establishment is strictly and jointly liable for damage caused by the services it provides (art. 23, § 7).
The tension in the model. A student boarding house, with a monthly price and long stays, is classified by IBGE as “non-tourist” accommodation and moves away from the temporary lodging defined in the Tourism Law. The further it moves, the harder it is to argue that it falls outside the Tenancy Law.
4 · Taxation

Is it possible to pay only 6% in taxes?

Only in the first bracket of the Simples Nacional, and only if the activity is genuine lodging. A building of ten studios at R$ 1,500 a month already earns R$ 180,000 a year — the ceiling of that bracket.

Why lodging can be in the Simples

Lodging is a service subject to ISS, the municipal service tax: item 9.01 of the list in Complementary Law No. 116/2003 expressly names boarding houses. Accommodation activities are taxed under Annex III of the Simples Nacional — Brazil’s simplified tax regime for small businesses — whose first bracket has a nominal rate of 6% up to R$ 180,000 of gross revenue over 12 months.

Leasing your own property is now excluded, with no exception

Change brought by Complementary Law No. 214/2025. Much of what is published still says the ban has an exception for services subject to ISS. That has changed. Complementary Law No. 214/2025 rewrote art. 17, XV, of Complementary Law No. 123/2006: the Simples is now barred to a company “that leases its own real estate,” with no exception. The change has applied since 1 January 2025 (art. 544, II, of Complementary Law No. 214).

The Federal Revenue Service’s Simples Q&A (Perguntas e Respostas), in its October 2026 version, still reproduces the old wording in questions 2.2 and 2.21. The document itself warns that it is being updated; the text of the law prevails.

In practice: genuine lodging remains a service, not a lease. But if the tax authority concludes that the operation is rent under another name, there is no longer any exception to keep it in the Simples — the result is removal from the regime and a demand for the unpaid tax.

The rate rises with revenue

In the Simples, the effective rate is (12-month revenue × nominal rate − deduction) ÷ 12-month revenue (Complementary Law No. 123, art. 18, § 1-A, and Annex III). The unit counts and prices below are illustrative.

Four cautions

  1. Registration does not decide the classification. The activity actually carried out does.
  2. 6% is only the first bracket. As the table shows, a mid-sized building already pays between 7% and 10%.
  3. The tax is levied on gross revenue, not on the profit left for the investor.
  4. In lodging, everything in the price is revenue. Electricity, water, internet, cleaning and linens enter the tax base; in a lease, these bills are usually paid directly by the tenant.

What the tax reform changes

  • Inside the Simples: from 2027, IBS and CBS — the new consumption taxes created by the reform — are collected in the DAS, the single monthly payment, in place of PIS and Cofins, and the regime’s nominal rates stay the same (Complementary Law No. 123, art. 18, § 1-C). Companies may choose to pay IBS and CBS outside the Simples, opting in September or March.
  • Outside the Simples: residential leases get a 70% reduction in IBS and CBS rates and a social deduction of R$ 600 per property per month (Complementary Law No. 214, arts. 260 and 261). Hotels and lodging get a 40% reduction, with no social deduction (arts. 277 and 278). Residential leases of up to 90 days follow the hotel rules (art. 253).
  • Individuals: only become liable for IBS and CBS on rent with revenue above R$ 240,000 a year and more than three properties (art. 251, § 1).

6 · Permits

Permits: what to check before you build?

The operating model must be defined before the design is approved: lodging and housing are different uses, with different requirements. Changing after the occupancy permit (habite-se) means new permits and, often, new building work.

  1. Zoning. Check the municipal zoning rules to confirm that lodging is allowed at the address. A lot approved for multi-family housing may not allow accommodation services.
  2. Fire safety. Under the São Paulo State Fire Safety Regulation (Decree No. 63,911/2018, Table 1), residential studios are class A-2; a boarding house with up to 16 beds is A-3; pensões and boarding houses with more than 16 beds are B-1. Ten units with double beds already add up to 20 beds — and the class sets the required measures.
  3. Accessibility. Hotels, inns and similar establishments must follow universal design (Decree No. 9,296/2018). For micro and small businesses, Decree No. 9,405/2018 applies: 5% of rooms accessible, at least one unit, and an accessible route. In a ten-studio building, that is an entire unit designed for wheelchair use.
  4. Operating license. A municipal permit for the lodging activity and, if meals are served, a health permit.
  5. Tourism registry. Cadastur, if the operation is a lodging establishment under the General Tourism Law. It does not replace the permits above.
  6. Tax setup. CNAE code, tax regime and filing obligations, defined with the accountant.

Permitting is part of the business plan: it changes the construction cost, the time until revenue starts and whether the business can operate at all.

7 · Returns

How to compare the returns of the two models?

Compare net results, not tax rates. Lodging’s tax advantage must comfortably cover the cost of the services it requires.

The 11.3% for companies under presumed profit combines corporate income tax, IRPJ (15% on a 32% presumed margin), CSLL (9% on 32%) and cumulative PIS/Cofins (3.65%). Valid for 2026: in 2027, PIS and Cofins give way to CBS.

Illustrative example — ten units

Hypothetical assumption: revenue of R$ 18,000 a month (R$ 216,000 a year) in both models. Lodging under the Simples: effective rate of 6.87%. Leasing through a company under presumed profit: about 11.33%. Difference: about R$ 9,600 a year, or R$ 80 per unit per month.

Those R$ 80 have to pay for each unit’s electricity, internet, cleaning, linens and management — and still leave a margin. If they don’t, the model with the lower tax rate earns less.

8 · Building system

Where does Light Steel Framing fit into this analysis?

The building system does not define the tax model or guarantee returns. It changes investment variables — timeline, repeatability, cost predictability — that need to be measured and compared.

Light Steel Framing can be used both for studio units for lease and for lodging projects. In projects with repeated units, panel modulation, early coordination of building services and planned assembly favor standardized execution.

Modular components and planned fabrication→Greater repeatability and less rework→More predictable cost and an earlier start of operations

It is a chain of possible consequences, not a promise: these results depend on the quality of the designs, crew training, logistics and material supply.

Three decisions in this report affect the modulation and must be settled before the panels are designed: the accessible unit, the building’s fire-safety class and the position of bathrooms and service areas. In any building system, changing them later costs more.

Acoustic performance between units, thermal performance and fire resistance belong to the complete system — structure, cladding, insulation, building services and execution details — not to the steel stud alone. In buildings with several units, acoustics between neighbors and fire safety deserve special attention.

9 · Strategy

When does each model make more sense?

Leasing suits those who want contractual income and little day-to-day operation; lodging suits those with proven demand for serviced stays and the structure to manage them.

Residential leaseRelatively predictable contractual income, low service intensity and long-term residents.
LodgingProvable demand for temporary stays, professional management, a suitable design and enough revenue to cover operating costs and risks.
Leasing the whole building to an operatorThe investor leases the entire property to a company that runs the lodging (Tenancy Law, art. 47, IV). The investor receives rent, outside the Simples; the operating risk stays with the operator.

Location, user profile, municipal rules and scale also matter. A project for students calls for different commercial and operational choices than one for business travelers or tourists.

10 · Conclusion

It is not just about taxes

Registering a studio building as a boarding house is not, by itself, a tax-saving strategy. The classification must reflect a real, permitted and properly structured activity — and since Complementary Law No. 214/2025, leasing one’s own property no longer has any way into the Simples.

More important than the lowest tax rate is the best net result for that plot, that audience and that investment. Before building, assess the intended use, design, permits, costs and revenue potential together.

The business model drives the design, not the other way around. Before deciding, validate the classification with a lawyer and an accountant, and the design with an engineer or architect.
