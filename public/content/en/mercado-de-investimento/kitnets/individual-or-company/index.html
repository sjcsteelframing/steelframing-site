Studio units · Investment structure

Studio units for rent: individual or company?

No structure pays less tax in every case. With up to 2 units and no other income, an individual pays zero income tax in 2026; with 10 units, a company pays R$ 1,600 to R$ 2,900 less per month.

Overview

The decision that comes before the land

Anyone planning a building of studio units — kitnets, as they are called in Brazil — usually starts with three questions: how much the land costs, how much the construction costs and how much rent the units can command. One earlier decision can change the result: will the properties be held by the investor as an individual (CPF) or by a company (CNPJ)?

The choice affects the tax on rent, administrative costs, how the assets are organized, succession and how the investment can grow. With Brazil’s tax reform, the math also changes over time: the company’s tax burden falls in 2027 and rises again by 2033.

The right question is not who pays less tax in isolation, but which structure leaves the best financial and asset result for that investment and that investor.

This page covers Brazilian law and Brazilian taxes. Amounts are in Brazilian reais (R$).

A simulation, not advice. The figures use 2026 rules and a standard case: rent of R$ 1,800 per unit, no vacancy. The investor’s income, how the properties are registered, the municipality and rules still being regulated all change the result. Check your own case with an accountant before deciding.

1 · Structure

What changes between building as an individual and as a company

Owning the property and running the rental are related but separate things. As an individual, the owner receives the rent and declares it in the personal income tax return. In a company, revenue, expenses and taxes go through the company’s books, under the tax regime it chooses.

A company helps organize partners, management and growth. But on its own it does not remove asset risk, does not guarantee tax savings and does not replace succession planning.

2 · Individuals

How rent is taxed for individuals

Rent received from individual tenants is calculated every month through the carnê-leão (monthly tax slip); when the tenant is a company, it withholds the tax at source. In the annual return, rent is added to the investor’s other taxable income.

Since January 2026, Law No. 15,270/2025 has created a reduction that brings the monthly tax to zero for taxable income of up to R$ 5,000 and reduces it partially up to R$ 7,350. In practice, someone with 1 or 2 studio units at R$ 1,800 and no other taxable income pays no income tax on the rent in 2026. In the annual return, the equivalent reduction brings the tax to zero for income of up to R$ 60,000 a year and reduces it up to R$ 88,200.

In the annual return, the investor can also replace all deductions with the standard deduction (desconto simplificado): 20% of taxable income, capped at R$ 17,640 for 2026. For someone living only on the rent, this is usually the better option, and the simulation below includes it.

Above that, the effective rate rises. Someone living only on the rent of 10 units (R$ 18,000 a month) pays about 20% of revenue, after the standard deduction. Someone who already has a salary or director’s fees pays 27.5% on practically all the rent, because the lower brackets are already used up by the other income.

What can be deducted

When paid by the landlord, these can be deducted from rent: property tax (IPTU) and other property fees, collection costs (such as a letting agent’s fee) and condominium fees. Maintenance, renovation, depreciation and loan interest cannot.

That is why the investment math is not the same as the tax math: an expense can reduce the project’s result without reducing the income tax base.

3 · Companies

How rent is taxed for companies

A company that rents out its own property cannot opt for the Simples Nacional, Brazil’s simplified tax regime for small businesses. The ban is in art. 17, XV, of Complementary Law No. 123/2006, which since 2025, as amended by Complementary Law No. 214/2025, has no exception. Opening a company to receive rent therefore does not lead to the 6% rate — that rate applies to genuine lodging, the subject of the boarding-house report.

Presumed profit

This is the regime most rental companies use. The law presumes that 32% of revenue is profit and taxes that base:

  • corporate income tax (IRPJ) of 15% (4.8% of revenue), plus a 10% surtax on presumed profit above R$ 20,000 a month — which only happens with revenue above R$ 62,500 a month;
  • CSLL of 9% (2.88% of revenue);
  • in 2026, cumulative PIS and Cofins of 3.65% of revenue.

Together they come to 11.33% of gross revenue below the surtax. Complementary Law No. 224/2025 raised the presumption percentages by 10% from 2026, but only on the part of annual revenue above R$ 5 million — well beyond a studio-unit building.

Actual profit

This regime taxes adjusted accounting profit. It may be worth studying when there are high expenses, losses or significant financing. High expenses alone are not enough: whether they are deductible and the cost of the bookkeeping have to be part of the math.

From the company to the partner

Distributed profits remain tax-exempt for the partner, with three cautions:

  • Bookkeeping. Without full accounting records, the tax-exempt distribution is limited to presumed profit minus taxes. In a 10-unit building, that is about R$ 3,700 a month out of cash of nearly R$ 16,000. Distributing the rest tax-free requires full accounting.
  • 10% withholding. Since January 2026 (Law No. 15,270/2025), when one company pays more than R$ 50,000 in profits to the same partner in a month, it withholds 10% income tax on the whole amount. With 20 units at R$ 1,800, the distribution is about R$ 32,000 a month, below that limit.
  • Minimum tax. Anyone with total income above R$ 600,000 a year now has a minimum income tax, reaching 10% from R$ 1.2 million; dividends count towards it.

The break-even point

If the company costs R$ 800 a month, it pays off from 8 units for someone living only on the rent, and from 3 for someone who already has other income. At R$ 1,200 a month, from 9 and from 5 units. Both costs are calculation assumptions.

The investor’s other income matters more than the number of units. The company cost used here is an assumption, not a quote: ask your accountant for yours.

What matters to the investor is the result after everything: actual revenue minus operating expenses, taxes and the cost of the structure — and, in a company, what actually reaches the partner.

5 · Tax reform

What the tax reform changes to 2033

The reform makes companies cheaper in 2027 and 2028 and more expensive by 2033; individuals with few units stay outside IBS and CBS.

From 2027, PIS and Cofins give way to CBS. From 2029 to 2032, IBS phases in, until the full model in 2033. IBS and CBS are the new consumption taxes created by the reform (a dual VAT). For property rental, Complementary Law No. 214/2025 cuts IBS and CBS rates by 70% and, for residential rent, allows a social deduction of R$ 600 per property from the tax base, indexed to inflation (arts. 260 and 261). Residential rentals of up to 90 days follow the hotel rules (art. 253).

For companies

A company is always a taxpayer. The estimate below uses the parameters the IBS Management Committee adopted in July 2026 to project revenue — CBS of 9.21% and IBS of 18.7%, which are not yet final rates —, with rent of R$ 1,800 and the deduction applied per unit.

If the whole building counts as a single property for the deduction, the 2033 burden rises to about 15.8%. The law defines a residential property as “the built unit” (art. 259, § 1, I), which supports reading it per unit; the regulations and the building’s land registration need to be checked.

For individuals

An individual only starts paying IBS and CBS on rent when, in the previous year, rental revenue exceeds R$ 240,000 and involves more than three distinct properties (art. 251). Within the year, the trigger is revenue above R$ 288,000, also with more than three properties (Decree No. 12,955/2026, art. 382). The amounts are indexed to inflation (IPCA).

  • 5 or 10 units at R$ 1,800 stay below R$ 240,000 a year: no IBS or CBS, whatever the number of land titles.
  • 20 units (R$ 432,000 a year) exceed the limit. If the units are distinct properties, the individual pays IBS and CBS on top of income tax.

Ten functional units are not necessarily ten distinct properties: each unit’s land registration and the way the building was registered are part of the analysis.

What may still change
  • IBS and CBS rates. The rates used here are the IBS Management Committee’s estimates for projecting revenue; the final rates have yet to be set.
  • Deduction per unit or per building. Reading it per unit depends on the regulations and the land registration. If the building counts as a single property, the company’s 2033 burden goes from about 13.3% to 15.8%.
  • ITBI on capital contributions. The Supreme Federal Court has decided Topic 1348, but the ruling has not been published; the final thesis and any limit on its effects are still pending.
6 · Holding company

Holding companies and transferring property

A “holding company” for assets is a company set up to own and manage property — it is not a tax regime. It can be used to centralize the management of several properties, set rules between partners, organize family holdings, plan succession and track a portfolio’s results.

It does not guarantee absolute asset protection or automatic savings. The result depends on corporate, tax, financial and succession factors.

The cost of moving property from an individual to a company

  • Income tax. An individual can transfer property into the company’s capital at the value declared in the income tax return, with no capital gain (Law No. 9,249/1995, art. 23). At market value, the difference is taxed as a gain.
  • ITBI (property transfer tax). The Constitution exempts capital contributions from ITBI, except for companies whose main activity is buying, selling or renting property (art. 156, § 2, I). On October 19, 2026, the Supreme Federal Court ruled, 8 votes to 2, that the immunity also applies to real estate companies (Topic 1348), up to the value of the capital contributed. At the time of writing, the ruling had not been published, and the final thesis and any limit on its effects were still pending; until then, municipalities may still charge it.
  • Registry. The capital contribution deed, the land registry entry and the amendment of the articles of association have their own costs.
7 · Planning

Build as an individual and switch to a company later?

The decision is worth more before buying the land than after construction. Buying and building as an individual and transferring years later requires valuing the properties, new registrations and analyzing the income tax and ITBI on the transfer. Setting up the company first, on the other hand, does not guarantee better financing or cheaper construction.

The choice affects:

  • financing for the land and construction, and the guarantees required;
  • how the construction is contracted;
  • whose name the finished building is in and how the units are registered — one land title for the building or one per unit;
  • the cost of a future transfer;
  • the taxation of the rental and of any future sale.

One land title per unit matters for the tax reform, the social deduction and the individual’s property count. If that is the route, it has to be planned from the approved design. For anyone planning more than one project, it is worth comparing structures before signing for the land.

8 · Building system

Where Light Steel Framing fits in

Individual or company defines how the investment is organized; the building system changes how the building is produced. They are different decisions that need to work together.

In buildings with repeated units, Light Steel Framing works with modular components and planned assembly. This can standardize stages and make cost and timeline more predictable — provided there is a coordinated design, a trained crew and coordinated supply.

Fast assembly does not, by itself, shorten the project timeline: permits, infrastructure and supply can delay the start of revenue. And the complete system must meet performance requirements — structure, fire, acoustics, thermal comfort and durability.

In the financial analysis, tax structure and building system are separate variables: an advantage that comes from how the investment is organized should not be credited to LSF, nor the other way around.

9 · Strategy

When each option makes more sense

IndividualA small portfolio, an investor without other high income and company costs that the tax difference does not cover. With up to 2 units and no other income, income tax is zero in 2026.
CompanyEnough scale — in the simulation, from 8 to 9 units for those living on the rent, or 3 to 5 for those with other income —, partners, growth plans and a full comparison that includes accounting and the reform.
Holding companySeveral assets, family or partners and succession to plan, with transfer and running costs justified by the benefits.

Ranges from the simulation, with rent of R$ 1,800 and company costs of R$ 800 to R$ 1,200 a month.

There is no universal number of units: the investor’s total income, how the properties are registered, costs and goals all change the result.

10 · Conclusion

The legal structure is part of feasibility

Building studio units as an individual or through a company can lead to different results even with the same land, design and rent. A company tends to pay off as revenue grows and when the investor already has other income; the reform lowers the company’s burden in 2027 and 2028 and raises it by 2033.

No structure guarantees returns. The choice between individual and company is part of the feasibility study, alongside design, construction cost, financing, operation and risk.

Decide the structure before buying the land. Check it with an accountant and a lawyer, and the design with an engineer or architect.
