Steel Framing vs Masonry: Schedule

A faster frame does not guarantee an earlier delivery.

Schedule

Assembly speed is not the same as project schedule

A structure that can be assembled quickly does not alone allow the conclusion that the entire project will be delivered sooner. It is necessary to distinguish frame assembly, construction duration, and total project schedule.

The potential of LSF appears when design, coordination, fabrication, procurement, logistics and assembly function as a coordinated process.

In summary

  • LSF can favor schedule reduction and predictability.
  • This depends on planning and supply chain.
  • Do not compare frame assembly with the complete construction work.
  • Late changes and rework can consume the expected gain.

Where time can be gained

Design defined→Preparation / fabrication→Logistics→Assembly→Cladding and finishes

Dry processes, pre-assembly and greater prior definition can reduce certain site activities. The real effect depends on the project and site organization.

Where Steel Framing can also be delayed

  • Incomplete or uncoordinated design.
  • Changes after fabrication or assembly.
  • Lack of components or suppliers.
  • Inadequate logistics.
  • Untrained team.
  • Rework at interfaces.

Compare equivalent schedules

The comparison must start from the same point and end at the same point: design, foundation, structure, cladding, installations, finishes and delivery condition. Comparing frame assembly with a complete conventional construction distorts the result.

When schedule becomes money

In properties intended for sale, rental, hospitality or operation, completing earlier can anticipate revenue or reduce certain costs. The consequence only exists when the schedule gain is real and the asset can begin operating.
