How Light Steel Framing Works

Panels, load paths, wall composition, dry construction and the distinction between LSF and Drywall — without oversimplification.

Structural panels

The basic unit is the structural panel

Light Steel Framing is organized around structural panels: assemblies of galvanized steel profiles connected to form a load-bearing plane. These panels make up walls, floors and roofs.

Each panel works as a composite system. Profiles, sheathing, connections and the way panels relate to one another determine structural behavior — not the individual component alone.

Load path

Loads travel through a defined structural path

In a Light Steel Framing building, vertical and horizontal loads are transferred through a continuous structural path: from roof to upper floors, down to the walls, foundation beams and finally to the foundation.

This continuity requires consistent detailing at every connection — from panel to panel, floor to floor, and wall to foundation. Structural integrity depends on the chain being complete.

Wall composition

The wall is a system of layers, not a single material

An external wall in Light Steel Framing is composed of multiple layers: structural profiles, sheathing boards, insulation, air and moisture barriers, and exterior cladding. Each layer has a specific function.

Thermal performance, acoustic behavior, fire resistance and durability all result from the composition of layers — not from any single element. This is why specifying the complete wall system matters as much as choosing the structural profiles.

Dry construction

Assembly without mortar or wet curing

Light Steel Framing is a dry construction system: profiles are cut and assembled with screws, without mortar, concrete pours or wet plastering. Finishing layers — boards, cladding, coatings — are applied dry or with minimal moisture.

This approach affects the construction process: it requires precision at the planning and execution stages, since corrections after assembly are more complex than in traditional wet systems.

LSF ≠ Drywall

Light Steel Framing is not the same as Drywall

Drywall is a finishing and partition system used in buildings of any structural type — concrete, masonry, steel, wood. It is not a structural system.

Light Steel Framing is a structural system in which cold-formed steel profiles carry the building's loads. Drywall boards can be used as sheathing within an LSF building, but the two terms refer to different things. Confusing them leads to incorrect expectations about structural capacity and performance.
